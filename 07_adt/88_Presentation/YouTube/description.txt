How can cardiac MRI analysis become more auditable? This research presentation connects image-derived descriptors to recognizable cardiac measurements and conflict-aware rules, with explicit abstention when evidence is insufficient.

The study evaluates 150 ACDC cases and 100 external M&Ms cases. It examines semantic reconstruction, rule coverage, uncertainty, predicate alignment, and sensitivity to small image perturbations.

Key findings include 86% held-out accuracy for a comparator using measured cardiac semantics, 64% coverage for the 200-rule audit, and a 2.25-fold increase in normalized reconstruction error on the external cohort. Rule balanced accuracy was 0.644 among non-abstained cases, highlighting why coverage must accompany selective performance.

Scope: the evaluated release uses deterministic image-mask descriptors and frozen training tertiles, rather than a trained deep encoder or the full weighted entropy-density discretization method. The findings support selective research auditing, not autonomous diagnosis or completed clinical validation.

Research manuscript:
Auditing Deep Learning for Cardiac MRI Through Semantic Transitions and Conflict-Aware Production Rules
