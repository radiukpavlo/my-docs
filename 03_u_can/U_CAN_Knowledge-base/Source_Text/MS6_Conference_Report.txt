Executive Summary
Conference overview and reporting summary
The International Conference “Reducing CO₂ Emissions in Ukrainian Cities: Pathways to Climate Neutrality” was organised by Khmelnytskyi National University (KhNU) on 24 April 2026 in Khmelnytskyi, Ukraine, in a hybrid format. The event was delivered within the Horizon Europe project “U_CAN – Ukraine towards Carbon Neutrality” [1] (Grant Agreement No. 101148374 [2]) and is reported here as evidence for the MS6 milestone, WP4 Task 4.5 implementation in Khmelnytskyi, and WP7 communication, dissemination and exploitation records (Table 1).

Milestone completion statement
This report documents the completion of Milestone MS6 through the organisation and delivery of the International Conference “Reducing CO₂ Emissions in Ukrainian Cities: Pathways to Climate Neutrality”, held on 24 April 2026 in Khmelnytskyi, Ukraine, in a hybrid format. The milestone is considered completed because the conference was delivered as a full-day knowledge-dissemination, stakeholder-engagement and municipal-exchange activity directly linked to WP4 Task 4.5, namely the Khmelnytskyi pilot on climate-neutral pilot activities, adaptive traffic management, transport-related CO₂ reduction, and local policy capacity.

Project and Conference Background
U_CAN supports Ukrainian cities in moving towards climate neutrality by aligning local urban transformation with European climate and smart-city priorities. Within WP4, pilot implementation in Ukraine is supported by European front-runners. For Khmelnytskyi, Task 4.5 links knowledge dissemination on CO₂ reduction with the development of an adaptive traffic management approach and city-level policy capacity.
The conference connected the technical KhNU pilot to the city's institutional needs. KhNU researchers and invited speakers addressed AI-supported traffic pattern recognition, topography-aware traffic signal control, digital modelling, sustainable urban mobility planning, and municipal implementation conditions. Organisation with Khmelnytskyi City Council and Strategic Alignment
The conference was organised by KhNU in collaboration with KhCC, which was listed as a co-organiser in the official agenda and represented municipal transport, infrastructure, public transport, and environmental services. Substantive Outcomes and Contribution to the Khmelnytskyi Pilot
The main substantive outcome was strengthened knowledge exchange on reducing transport-related CO₂ emissions in Ukrainian cities. The conference increased visibility of U_CAN and of the KhNU pilot; disseminated European approaches to low-emission mobility; presented Ukrainian municipal practices; and created a structured policy bridge between the Khmelnytskyi 2035 Strategy [6] and practical adaptive traffic management works [7], [8].
The technical framing is supported by open-access KhNU-linked research on adaptive machine learning for sustainable traffic planning and topography-aware traffic control. These publications are cited as scientific background for the pilot, not as measured impacts of the conference itself. Ethics, GDPR and Consent
Identifiable photographs, video frames, registration records, feedback records, and follow-up contact preferences are treated as personal data. KhNU, as the local event organiser and evidence holder for this milestone report, retains the raw registration and feedback materials in a restricted internal evidence archive in accordance with the project’s data-management procedures. Access to raw personal data is limited to authorised project staff who require access for reporting, verification, audit, dissemination-permission management, or follow-up communication where consent has been provided.
The report uses only aggregate or anonymised participant and feedback information. Personal contact details, individual feedback entries, raw registration records, and identifiable consent records are not reproduced in the public version of the report. Follow-up communication will be sent only to participants who provided permission for contact or where another valid project basis exists under the applicable data-management procedures. Identifiable images and recordings may be reused only where the registration/consent record, event notice, or project communication permissions support such reuse.
The controlled-access evidence archive should retain the registration workbook, feedback workbook, consent records, photo/video permission evidence, and video files for verification purposes. If a project review, audit, or granting-authority request requires access, the relevant documents should be provided through the coordinator or according to the project’s agreed reporting procedure.
Conclusion
The International Conference “Reducing CO₂ Emissions in Ukrainian Cities: Pathways to Climate Neutrality” was successfully organised and conducted by Khmelnytskyi National University on 24 April 2026, confirming the achievement of its milestone and dissemination objectives within the U_CAN project. Throughout the opening session, university and municipal representatives framed climate neutrality as a shared responsibility that requires cooperation among research, city administration, and public service actors. The plenary contributions linked U_CAN’s Ukrainian pilot work with European experience in sustainable urban mobility, zero-carbon districts and integrated energy-mobility systems.
Session I disseminated European approaches to low-emission mobility, including sustainable urban mobility planning, governance, public engagement and cross-city learning. Session II translated these ideas into the Ukrainian municipal context, highlighting transport management, electric public transport, urban infrastructure, spatial planning and practical experience from Khmelnytskyi, Vinnytsia and Lviv. Session III connected citizen engagement, geoengineering, smart environmental management, public administration and KhNU’s AI-based adaptive traffic-management research, showing that future emission-reduction measures depend on both technical innovation and institutional capacity.
The conference achieved its milestone purpose by delivering a documented dissemination, stakeholder-engagement and municipal-exchange activity under WP4 Task 4.5, with links to WP7 communication, dissemination and exploitation. It increased the visibility of U_CAN, strengthened cooperation between KhNU and KhCC, disseminated European and Ukrainian low-emission mobility practices, and created an evidence-based basis for future pilot uptake. Finally, the conference report documents enabling conditions for subsequent Strategy-aligned, data-informed and replicable climate-neutral mobility actions in Ukrainian cities.
