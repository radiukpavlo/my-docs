Slide 1: WP4: Pilot Implementation in Ukraine, supported by European front-runners
Welcome everyone to this U_CAN Consortium Meeting update; I am Pavlo Radiuk, and today I will present the progress our Khmelnytskyi National University team achieved throughout March 2026. This month was fruitful for us; we focused extensively on advancing our topography-aware traffic control research and rigorously preparing for the upcoming national conference on carbon neutrality. These combined efforts represent milestones for our overarching project goals.
Slide 2: Manuscript Acceptance and Methodological Framing
I am thrilled to report that a high-impact, formally accepted our research manuscript, a Q2 peer-reviewed journal, and it will be available online shortly. On the 16th and 17th of March, we successfully revised the introduction and methodological framing to clearly address how generic reinforcement learning rewards fail in hilly urban corridors. This specific revision greatly strengthened the core argument of our paper by directly targeting reductions in carbon dioxide emissions.
Slide 3: Formal Model and Reward Design Updates
Following that, between the 18th and 19th of March, we significantly updated our formal model and the Context-Specific Reward Design procedure. By separating static geometric information from dynamic traffic flow descriptors, our revised model dynamically penalises energy-intensive uphill restarts. This successfully addresses a major environmental blind spot inherent in traditional, flat-geometry traffic models.
Slide 4: Simulation Framework and Calibration Workflow
We refined our simulation framework and empirical calibration workflow using video-derived traffic data for a complex four-way intersection. We successfully simulated 14,800 vehicles over a nine-hour horizon to properly test our continuous deep reinforcement learning loop. Crucially, this included integrating a realistic twenty per cent heavy-vehicle share on a challenging 3.8-degree uphill approach.
Slide 5: Finalisation, Statistical Validation, and Dissemination
By the 24th of March, we finalised the statistical validation of our controller, achieving a notable 8.6 per cent reduction in total carbon dioxide emissions compared to the baseline. We also secured a minimised average trip duration of 72.09 seconds, maintaining an average queue length of just 1.31 vehicles. The very next day, we transferred our results to highlight the validated adaptive traffic-management solution for the city of Khmelnytskyi.
