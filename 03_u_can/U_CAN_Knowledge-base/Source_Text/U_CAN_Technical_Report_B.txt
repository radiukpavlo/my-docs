1. EXPLANATION OF THE WORK CARRIED OUT AND OVERVIEW OF THE
     PROGRESS

Ukraine towards Carbon Neutrality (U_CAN) is a Coordination and Support Action funded un-
der the EU Framework Programme for Research and Innovation, Horizon Europe, and contributes
to the EU Mission Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities. U_CAN supports selected Ukrainian cities in
aligning their strategies and standards with EU climate policies. This is achieved through targeted
support to Ukrainian city administrations in their efforts to collaborate with European counterparts,
including EU Mission Cities.

This section provides an overview of the work carried out and progress achieved during the first
reporting period (months 1-18) of the U_CAN project. Progress is reported against the project ob-
jectives as defined in the Description of Action (DoA) and focuses on activities implemented to date.
The section introduces the main areas of work, including twinning activities, pilot preparation, ca-
pacity building, impact, and dissemination, which are described in more detail in the subsequent
subsections.

1.1 Objectives

Objective 1: to support Ukrainian cities that are willing to transition to climate neutrality by devel-
oping context specific climate strategies. The envisioned outcome is a core group / network of pio-
neering Ukrainian stakeholders who require support in building capacities to address climate neu-
trality.

Progress:
    - Six Ukrainian cities (Lviv, Kyiv, Zhytomyr, Khmelnytskyi, Ivano-Frankivsk, Vinnytsia)
         were engaged as a structured pilot group for climate-neutral transition activities. Lviv, Zhy-
         tomyr, and Vinnytsia participate through direct municipal representatives, while Kyiv,
         Khmelnytskyi, and Ivano-Frankivsk are represented through university partners and NGOs
         respectively. In all cases, designated municipal contact persons were identified, involved in
         all key workshops, and engaged in bilateral discussions with project partners.
    - City-specific needs, priorities, and institutional capacities were collected through structured
         exchanges involving representatives from each pilot city, including Knowledge and Expe-
         rience Exchange Sessions (KEES) (2 synergy workshops and over 20 bilateral meetings), 2
         extensive questionnaires, 3 thematic workshops led by Mission Cities, 5 structured inter-
         views, and 2 dedicated workshops. These activities were implemented by multiple project
         partners across different research areas, as detailed in the respective Work Package descrip-
         tions.

Objective 2: to identify key sectors in Ukraine for climate transition. This includes identifying crit-
ical sectors that require transitioning including industrial decarbonisation, energy transition, nature-
based solutions, etc. Outcomes envisioned here are recommendations, standards and best practices
co-developed with EU experts.

Progress:
    - Key climate transition sectors were identified at national and municipal levels through a
         combination of policy analysis, scientific literature review, and structured inputs from the
         six Ukrainian pilot cities. This work resulted in the completion of Deliverable D3.1 - Sec-
         tors for Climate Actions in Ukraine, which provides an integrated assessment of priority
         sectors, policy relevance, local capacities, and barriers.
    - Based on this sectoral mapping, research tasks under WP5 have been initiated and advanced,
         analysing standardisation, modernisation, and technological needs across the identified sec-
         tors. This includes the completion of Deliverable D5.1 - Overview of the standardisation
         landscape and ongoing analytical work on building renovation, energy systems, industrial

         decarbonisation, and local climate governance constraints, providing a concrete basis for
         translating sectoral priorities into technical and procedural pathways.

Objective 3: to facilitate CNSC twinning activities, fostering the exchange of best practices in cli-
mate action. This facilitation includes connecting CNSC Mission cities as pioneering examples
and mentors for Ukrainian cities to achieve climate neutrality. This leadership will leverage the ex-
pertise and success stories of established CNSC cities to inspire and support their Ukrainian coun-
terparts. The envisioned outcomes are long-term partnerships between CNSC Mission cities and
partner Ukrainian cities.

Progress:
    - Partnerships were formally established with six Mission Cities (Bratislava, Dresden,
         Kosice, Leipzig, Thessaloniki, and Wroclaw), to support our Ukrainian pilot cities in their
         transition to climate neutrality.
    - A structured twinning framework between Ukrainian pilot cities and EU CNSC Mission
         Cities was established and operationalised through the design and implementation of the
         Knowledge and Experience Exchange Sessions (KEES).
    - Twinning activities were implemented through multiple exchange formats, including 2
         CNSC Synergy Workshops (1 online and 1 hybrid at Wroclaw), over 20 bilateral meetings
         between Ukrainian and Mission City representatives, and 3 thematic workshops address-
         ing priority topics such as sectoral pathways, solar and low-carbon solutions, and public
         engagement. These activities enabled targeted knowledge transfer, needs clarification, and
         the assessment of compatibility for longer-term city-to-city cooperation.

Objective 4: to facilitate local Pilot initiatives as indication of on-ground efforts demonstrating
feasibility and benefits of climate-neutral measures. This will also propagate tangible efforts in
terms of replication and knowledge exchange between Ukrainian partner cities and other cities in
Ukraine. Outcomes envisioned here are localised pilot implementation plans.

Progress:
    - Task 4.1 progressed through the development and application of a common framework for
         climate-neutrality indicators in pilot cities. A preliminary set of indicators was defined using
         a combined methodological approach, drawing on international sustainability assessment
         frameworks and adapted to the Ukrainian context. Indicators were reviewed and prioritised
         by representatives of all six pilot cities based on relevance and data availability, supported
         by workshops, questionnaires, and bilateral meetings. By the end of the reporting period,
         Milestone MS5 (Indicators for climate neutrality in the context of pilot activities) was
         achieved, and the preparation of Deliverable D4.1 was underway.
    - Across Tasks 4.2-4.7, pilot implementation activities were initiated in all six pilot cities,
         focusing on city-specific priority areas and the preparation of local pilot concepts, in coor-
         dination with municipal stakeholders and European partners. These activities prompted the
         gradual development of the pilot concepts, institutional arrangements, and stakeholder en-
         gagement processes that form the basis for localised pilot implementation plans.

Objective 5: to facilitate awareness-building among a wider group of Ukrainian cities. This will
ensure the dissemination and exploitation of project results for long-term impacts on Ukraine's align-
ment with the EGD. Outcome envisioned here is exploitation of the project activities to pan-Ukraine
CNSC initiatives.

Progress:
    - Project results and activities were actively disseminated to a wider group of Ukrainian and
         European stakeholders through the establishment and operation of the U_CAN project plat-
         form and associated communication channels. During the reporting period, the bilingual
         project website was launched and regularly updated, supported by interactive maps, news

         access to project information beyond the project partners and the six pilot cities.

    - Communication and dissemination materials (brochures, postcards, digital banners, videos)

         results, strengthening visibility and engagement with Ukrainian cities beyond the core pilot

    - Engagement with the EU Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities Mission ecosystem was initiated

         of a public group within the NetZeroCities Platform to share U_CAN activities and outputs,

         the project.

1.2.3. Work Package 3: CNSC synergy building towards climate goals (M1-M48, WUST)

WP3 Objectives

The aim of this WP is to initiate and conduct twinning among the CNSC mission cities and other
EU entities with collaboration-minded Ukrainian stakeholders involved in localised climate action
initiatives. The objectives are:

    - Develop consensus building in envisioning climate goals in various planning and recon-
         struction sectors in Ukraine and initiate teaming and partnerships between Ukrainian and
         CNSC Mission cities.

    - Develop pilot Use cases for the Ukrainian cities of Lviv, Kyiv, Zhytomyr, Khmelnytskyi,
         Ivano Frankivsk and Vinnytsia that aspire climate neutrality.

    - Support local authorities of pilot cities in building capacities for climate policy dialogues
         and creating opportunities for consensus-building with local communities and other stake-
         holder groups, including determining the economic and social benefits of the green trans-
         formation for all potential stakeholders and

    - Collaborate with the Mission platform and identify online networking opportunities for col-
         laboration with funding institutes, and additional stakeholders in interested Ukrainian cities
         with other EU organisations by network building to link Ukrainian cities with EU and UN
         climate goals.

Progress in WP3 during the first Reporting Period of the project

WP3 focused on identifying priority sectors for climate action in Ukraine and initiating structured
knowledge exchange between Ukrainian pilot cities and EU Mission Cities. The work package de-
livered key analytical inputs on sectoral priorities and launched the twinning process through tar-
geted exchange formats, laying the foundation for capacity building, pilot implementation, and long-
term cooperation in support of climate neutrality objectives.

Achievements:

Task 3.1 was planned for the first 12 months of the project and entailed (i) the identification of
priority climate mitigation and adaptation sectors at the national and municipal levels and (ii) spatial
assessment of renewable energy deployment conditions. Two submitted deliverables associated with
the task are: (a) sectoral mapping of emissions and policy relevance (D3.1), and (b) geospatial suit-
ability analysis for solar power investment siting (D3.2).

The task was led by AU, who was also the lead author of D3.1, with contributions and reviews from
ENV, ULA, as well as WUST, JRG, and SFPA, while the principal authorship of D3.2 was held by
ISKPI, with contributions and reviews from ZCC and JRG.

Q2-Q5. The Deliverable D3.1. Sectors for Climate Actions in Ukraine was based on document anal-
ysis of national climate legislation, policy frameworks, and local climate plans, complemented by a
review of scientific literature and international best practice sources. Evidence was further informed
through bilateral agreement reviews and targeted searches in multiple languages to capture non-
English sources. Additionally, qualitative data were collected from six Ukrainian pilot cities through
interviews, a workshop, and an open-ended questionnaire conducted in spring-summer 2025, offer-
ing insights into local priorities, barriers, and institutional capacities. This combination of desk re-
search and stakeholder consultation enabled a multi-level assessment of climate action needs and
sectoral opportunities in Ukraine.

According to the report, Ukraine's climate policy targets climate neutrality by 2050 and a 65% re-
duction in GHG emissions by 2030, framing green reconstruction as essential for EU integration
and energy security. The energy sector is the core priority, alongside heating modernisation, renew-
able expansion, transport decarbonisation and waste management. In the six pilot cities, actions fo-
cus on heating upgrades, clean mobility, and resilience, and gain the strongest support when linked
to energy security. Key barriers include limited financing, weak coordination and administrative
capacity, and delays in the implementation of national instruments, all of which are amplified by
wartime conditions. Despite this, strong city leadership, EU alignment, academic cooperation and
public support create favourable conditions, with pilot initiatives expected to build capacity and
demonstrate scalable solutions.

Q2-Q4. The Deliverable D3.2. Ukrainian Territory Suitability for Solar Power Station Placement
employed an integrated, multi-criteria geospatial approach to evaluate land suitability for solar
power stations across Ukraine. It utilised satellite-derived climatic variables (GHI, temperature, pre-
cipitation, wind), topographic datasets (elevation and slope), and a 2022 national land-cover map.
All inputs were standardised through fuzzy logic to a 0-1 suitability scale. Their relative significance
was determined using the Analytical Hierarchy Process and expert pairwise comparison, yielding
consistent weighting coefficients. These weighted factors were then combined using the Weighted
Sum Model to produce a national suitability index, with wetlands, rivers, and dense forests entirely
excluded from potential zones. This process produced a spatially explicit suitability map that sup-
ports evidence-based identification of optimal solar PV deployment sites.

The geospatial assessment indicates that most of Ukraine's territory has medium to high suitability
for solar PV development, with the highest values concentrated in the southern oblasts and Crimea,
where suitability exceeds 0.75. Mountainous and densely forested areas, especially the Carpathians
and northern Polissia, are mainly unsuitable due to steep terrain and low solar access. Existing large
solar farms are mainly situated in zones classified as highly or very highly suitable, validating the
model. Yet, several regions (including Poltava, Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Luhansk, and northern
Odesa) remain underexploited despite their high potential. Notably, 35.65% of land falls within the
medium suitability class, 14.4% within the high suitability class, and 4.78% within the very high
suitability class, revealing significant potential to expand decentralised PV systems to support na-
tional energy security and climate neutrality objectives.

During the reporting period, the Kyiv pilot team developed the morphological analysis method,
modified by the authors, and adapted to the complex problems of planning cities with climate neu-

multiple influence criteria, and can be viewed as a part of an important process of transforming the
scientific methods into management decisions.

Q4-Q5. The authors tested 8 automobile tunnels provided for by the General Plan of Kyiv. The
obtained car tunnel prioritization for Kyiv city was a decision with several groups of risk (logistical,
ecological, safety) with different approaches to form rankings, that enhances management capabili-
ties. The morphological model allowed to assess the impact of 10 structural and functional factors,
and process ca. 280 000 value configurations to obtain numerical estimates for tunnel rankings. The
analysis of results reveals the leaders of the ranking, recommended for priority construction. De-
pending on the impact factor consideration, those are either tunnels 5, 4, 3 (approach 1), or tunnels
5, 7, 8 (approach 2), with tunnel 5 being the leader in both cases. Introduction of the climate neu-
trality factor to the set of the impact factors significantly influenced the tunnel prioritization results
and provided more reliable results for the future development of Kyiv city. This approach could be
an important factor in making management decisions for cities that are undergoing a transformation
of the transport system on the basis of climate neutrality.

Task 5.3: Procedures for repurposing war-affected demolition materials
M10-M33; ULA (RCSD, OMB, ENV)

Achievements:

Task 5.3 focuses on three main areas:
    - Debris management, with particular emphasis on selective demolition.
    - Recovery and reuse of building components.
    - Recycling of materials from decommissioned buildings.

Q4. A dedicated, iterative methodology was developed by ULA collaboratively with all task partners
and refined based on feedback from pilot cities and the preparatory Ukraine Reconstruction Confer-
ence (Rome, July 2025). Each phase was revisited and adjusted according to the outcomes of sub-
sequent steps, allowing continuous optimisation of analyses, decision-making, and intervention
strategies. The methodology comprised seven interrelated phases:

    - Characterization of typical buildings in the Ukrainian building stock, focusing on construc-
         tion techniques and materials.

    - Parametric quantification of materials and building components per unit of surface area.
    - Development of an algorithm to identify representative building types through cartographic

         analysis.
    - Statistical correlation of building types with material quantities.
    - Selection of best practices and definition of strategies for selective demolition.
    - Assessment of component reuse potential and recycling pathways for non-reusable materi-

         als.
    - Development of a framework for building waste management, accounting for recoverable

         materials.

This iterative approach ensures continuous refinement of procedures and supports evidence-based
strategies for the sustainable repurposing of materials from war-affected buildings.

Q5. Building on this, ULA conducted a detailed characterization of construction techniques and
materials in typical buildings and developed a parametric table quantifying the prevalence of domi-
nant materials and components (e.g., doors and windows, cladding, concrete panels). This work was
carried out in synergy with task 5.4.

To gather detailed information on the typological, material, and construction characteristics of build-
ings, two questionnaires were distributed to pilot cities in close collaboration with task 5.4, and co-
creation labs were held in July 2025 for more in-depth analysis, with the participation of interested
partners. These co-creation labs built on those held in Wroclaw, during the Consortium Meeting. A
comprehensive bibliographic review provided additional insights into Ukraine's architectural herit-
age.

Q6. The current integration phase focuses on implementing parametric calculation within a GIS
environment, based on the methodology previously developed in spreadsheets. In collaboration with
task partners, research was also focused on identifying best practices for selective demolition, com-

1.3 Impact

U_CAN presented five Activities envisioning Impacts towards the vision within the DoA:

(a) Mapping of Ukrainian cities Long-term Ukrainian City matches for Climate initiatives

During the first reporting period, U_CAN successfully advanced the identification and engagement
of Ukrainian cities committed to climate neutrality. This process confirmed cities' alignment with
EU climate objectives and supported the identification of priority sectors requiring targeted support,
thereby establishing a strong basis for meaningful participation in the Climate-Neutral and Smart
Cities (CNSC) Mission.

    - Five pilot cities--Lviv, Kyiv, Zhytomyr, Khmelnytskyi, and Ivano-Frankivsk--were iden-
         tified as the core group. Relevant representatives from each city administration are formally
         engaged to implement the planned pilot activities and to advance their cities' long-term cli-
         mate neutrality visions.

    - Partnerships were established with the City of Kharkiv, which joined the Network of Exter-
         nal Partners (NEP) and invited to key project workshops.

    - Collaboration was initiated with national and regional networks, including the Energy Ef-
         ficient Cities of Ukraine (EECU) association, providing access to a broader pool of munic-
         ipalities. Through these networks, additional interested cities will be engaged as network
         partners in subsequent phases of the project to support the continuation and scaling of cli-
         mate neutrality actions.

(b) Supporting Ukrainian cities in the sectors identified Strategic sectoral empowerment of cities
in UA

U_CAN strengthened strategic sectoral support for Ukrainian cities by expanding its engagement
beyond consortium expertise and systematically building an extended expert ecosystem around the
identified priority sectors.

    - The Network of External Partners (NEP) was established and is being expanded to include
         additional European cities, sector experts, and organisations with relevant experience in cli-
         mate-neutral transition, reconstruction, and sectoral innovation, providing Ukrainian cities
         with access to a broader pool of knowledge and practical support.

    - Liaison with CEN/TC 465, "Sustainable and Smart Cities and Communities," was estab-
         lished, strengthening alignment between Ukrainian city needs and European standardisation
         processes in key sectors related to sustainable transformation and climate neutrality.

(c) Twinning and teaming Ukraine with CNSC Mission cities  Long-term CNSC partnerships

As described in detail in the WP3 section, U_CAN advanced structured twinning and teaming be-
tween Ukrainian cities and CNSC Mission Cities. Twinning activities were operationalised through
multiple interaction formats, including 3 Synergy Workshops and over 20 bilateral meetings. These
collaborations enabled Ukrainian cities to access practical experience, tested solutions, and govern-
ance approaches from European counterparts, while also facilitating reciprocal knowledge exchange
with Mission Cities.

(d) Facilitating Use case based Pilot initiatives  Feasible adaptation of climate actions in
Ukraine

As outlined in the WP3 and WP4 descriptions, U_CAN initiated the development of use case-based
pilot initiatives derived from twinning collaborations between Ukrainian cities and CNSC Mission

and twinning exchanges into localised pilot concepts tailored to the contexts of the six Ukrainian
pilot cities (Lviv, Kyiv, Zhytomyr, Khmelnytskyi, Ivano-Frankivsk, and Vinnytsia). The pilot im-

(e) Disseminating and Exploiting best practices, success stories and CNSC collaboration  Cli-

During the reporting period, dissemination and communication activities focused on establishing
visibility, accessibility, and knowledge-sharing channels through the U_CAN project website, com-
munication materials, events, and engagement with European and international networks, as detailed
in the WP7 descriptions. These initial actions targeted city administrations, decision-makers, prac-
titioners, and civil society, creating a foundation for broader climate consciousness across sectors.
In parallel, U_CAN engaged with European and international initiatives and platforms, including
NetZeroCities, extending the reach and visibility of Ukrainian cities and opening pathways for future
integration with European Green Deal-aligned processes. While the systemic exploitation of best
practices and large-scale uptake will intensify in later project phases, the groundwork for long-term
dissemination, replication, and integration of climate-neutral approaches in Ukraine was established
during the first reporting period.

1.4 Update of the plan for exploitation and dissemination of results

During the reporting period, the consortium maintained the core objectives and target groups defined
in the original Plan for communication, dissemination and exploitation, while refining approaches
in response to stakeholder feedback and practical experience. The main updates relate to stronger
use of multimedia formats, bilingual dissemination materials tailored to Ukrainian and European
audiences, and a more practice-oriented design of key events to better support municipal needs.

Enhanced multimedia and dissemination materials

The project expanded its use of video beyond what was initially foreseen, creating a dedicated
YouTube channel for convenient storage, referencing and re-use of audiovisual content. In addition
to general project clips, new videos were produced to showcase partners' impressions from events
and to highlight their achievements in specific time frames, thereby supporting ongoing visibility
and exploitation of project results.

The consortium also broadened its portfolio of visual and printed materials in response to the needs
of pilot cities and stakeholders. Digital banners were developed to represent U_CAN pilot cities at
conferences and other events; and additional postcards were produced: a general postcard explaining
U_CAN's primary aims in accessible language plus city-specific postcards describing each pilot
city's goals. Produced in both English and Ukrainian, these materials have become essential tools
for conferences, webinars and meetings, enabling clear and targeted communication of project ob-
jectives and emerging results.

Central European Energy Conference: strategic adjustment

In the case of the Central European Energy Conference, the planned activity was adjusted from a
predominantly high-level, policy-oriented format to a more practice-oriented and bottom-up event
concept. This shift responded to feedback from municipalities and local actors, who expressed a
need for implementation-focused discussions, peer learning and concrete exchange on local climate
and energy solutions. The revised format strengthened the role of municipal networks and partners,

4. OPEN SCIENCE

The research activities within the project involved local stakeholders in the design of the pilot activ-
ities and were based on the local needs of the pilot municipalities and their respective stakeholders.
Key findings were presented to the pilot municipalities in thematic workshops, and other European
and Ukrainian stakeholders were also involved. The public results were compiled and will be in-
cluded in the project platform for knowledge exchange and networking. The first data sets and re-
sults have been included in the EU's open research repository, Zenodo, among others. This means
that various stakeholders have already contributed to the process and implementation of open sci-
ence practices at an early stage, including the exchange of knowledge and contacts with U_CAN's
sister project, SUN4Ukraine, and the Mission Platform.
