D4.5 – Implementation Plan Khmelnytskyi (KhNU)
Pilot focus: Adaptive traffic management for transport-related CO₂ reduction in Khmelnytskyi.
D4.5 status: Implementation-plan evidence base established; final D4.5 scheduled for Month 39.
Scientific validation: Validated traffic-pattern recognition using Khmelnytskyi transport-network simulation.
Research output: Published a Q2 Gold Open Access paper on adaptive machine learning for sustainable traffic planning.
Simulation evidence: Demonstrated scenario identification accuracy of approximately 92.8–95.0%.
CO₂ modelling result: Demonstrated simulation-based CO₂ reduction potential of up to 8.6% versus Max-Pressure and 4.7% versus standard DQN.
Municipal cooperation: Maintained cooperation with Khmelnytskyi City Council on climate indicators, transport data needs, and future policy integration.
Capacity building and dissemination: Delivered PTV Visum training with Khmelnytskyi City Council and organised the MS6 conference as a stakeholder-engagement milestone.
Primary Goal: To improve urban mobility efficiency and reduce CO₂ emissions.

D4.6 – Implementation Plan Ivano-Frankivsk
By June 2026 the pilot is firmly under way. Given wartime conditions and tight municipal budgets, the team adopted a pragmatic “soft” entry point: rather than starting from a single new hardware installation, it built on existing PV stations on public buildings in Ivano-Frankivsk, consolidating them under a unified, public monitoring system while the host building for the full pilot solution is finalised. The progress below relates to the Ivano-Frankivsk pilot
Public online monitoring platform (launched and operational) 
A public online monitoring platform for PV installations on public buildings has been launched and is live at asempv.escoua.com/en/p/ucan  . It provides real-time data on inverter performance, energy generation, the location of installations and operational statistics over time, increasing transparency and accountability and making technical energy data accessible to citizens, the City Council and international partners and donors.
Objectives: The implementation plan will detail the climate-neutral solutions and approaches derived from this pilot activity It is specifically designed to verify the effectiveness of renewable energy source integration, monitor energy generation, raise awareness among stakeholders, and co-develop policy recommendations to cater to the scope of scale-up and replication of such installations throughout the city

D4.6 – Implementation Plan Ivano-Frankivsk
Problems and potential missed deadlines
Wartime conditions and budget constraints. Procuring and installing a brand-new PV solution as originally scoped is challenging under current conditions. The pilot has adapted by first leveraging existing public-building PV assets and the monitoring platform; the climate-neutral approach and the Implementation Plan (D4.6) remain achievable, but the timing of new hardware procurement depends on the security and financing situation.
Power outages and grid instability. Prolonged blackouts disrupted platform operation in late 2025; the issue was resolved with the contractor (Jan 2026), but recurring outages remain a risk to continuous real-time monitoring.
Dependence on third-party data and the contractor. Connecting further installations relies on permissions from PV-station owners (e.g. the university) and on contractor availability for platform development, historical-data handling and usability — both require ongoing coordination.
Mitigation. The team is maintaining close alignment with the City Council, the municipal green deal and the SECAP to 2030, keeping the indicator and monitoring work ahead of schedule, and planning a contingency for new PV hardware procurement so that the Implementation Plan and the July 2027 EC deliverable are protected.
http://asempv.escoua.com/en/p/ucan
