Slide 1: KhNU Pilot Team
Dear colleagues, welcome everyone to this presentation detailing the recent progress of the Khmelnytskyi National University pilot team within the Horizon Europe U_CAN project. Today, I will guide you through our journey from final conference readiness to public dissemination, feedback gathering, and pilot-uptake planning.
Slide 2: April-May progress at a glance
This timeline provides a clear chronological overview of our activities throughout April and May. Our systematic approach moved explicitly from initial preparation and delivery to evidence archiving, public communication, and essential follow-up actions.
Slide 3: Final package ready before 24 April
In the crucial days leading up to the milestone, we ensured comprehensive venue readiness and a robust U_CAN visual identity. We also meticulously prepared the agenda, moderation flow, QR forms, and hybrid access parameters to guarantee seamless speaker coordination before the event.
Slide 4: KhNU delivered the U_CAN conference
On the 24th of April, our team successfully delivered the hybrid MS6 conference. We maintained a dynamic programme flow that actively engaged both the in-room audience and remote participants through a highly professional hybrid format.
Slide 5: Hybrid access connected European practice
By integrating hybrid access, we successfully connected our in-room participants with leading European practice and remote mobility inputs. This setup allowed us to share invaluable remote expertise regarding low-emission mobility, sustainable urban mobility planning logic, and practical city implementations.
Slide 6: Research and city learning aligned
During the conference, we successfully aligned academic research with practical city learning by engaging key stakeholders and municipal functions. This collaborative environment facilitated excellent media visibility, active participation from our European partners, and valuable Ukrainian peer learning.
Slide 7: Adaptive traffic pilot story
Our technical narrative focused heavily on the adaptive traffic pilot story, incorporating detailed modelling and the local city context. The ensuing follow-up route rests entirely on technical readiness and enabling conditions, strictly avoiding any unsupported claims about direct emissions reduction.
Slide 8: KhNU turned outputs into U_CAN evidence
Moving into late April and early May, we consolidated our archives to transform our outputs into concrete U_CAN evidence. All public posts, website pages, QR materials, and media files were systematically organised to fulfil our specific WP7 and MS6 dissemination requirements.
Slide 9: May evidence confirmed relevance
The feedback reviewed between the 7th and 10th of May strongly confirmed the relevance and success of our efforts. We achieved an exceptional overall rating of 4.75 out of 5, experienced zero quality assurance issues, and secured unanimous support for further U_CAN cooperation.
Slide 10: Pilot-uptake route set for May
To conclude, our pilot-uptake route for May transitions directly from dissemination into actionable technical steps. This strategic pathway includes final reporting, a municipal technical meeting, controlled material sharing, and further European and Ukrainian peer exchanges focussed on an adaptive traffic-management pathway.
