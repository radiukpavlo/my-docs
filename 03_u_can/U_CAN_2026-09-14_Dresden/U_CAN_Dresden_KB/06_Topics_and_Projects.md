## 1. Ukrainian projects presented in Dresden

### 1.1 U_CAN pilot — Adaptive Traffic Management (KhNU)
**Owner: Pavlo Radiuk.** Day 02 topic.

The U_CAN pilot for Khmelnytskyi. A Scenario Building workshop (Pavlo + the Deputy Mayor of
Khmelnytskyi) produced four plausible futures based on two axes — **AI-infrastructure affordability**
and **long-term funding security** — and their effect on implementation, stakeholder roles and
upscaling. The Dresden session stress-tests these against a technology shock
(see [05_Radiuk_Role_and_Content.md](05_Radiuk_Role_and_Content.md)).

KhNU's declared technical interests: AI-based modelling, simulation, digital mobility solutions,
adaptive traffic management, multimodal traffic management, public-transport priority.

### 1.2 Smart Thermochemical Conversion of Polymer Waste into Fuel (KhNU)
**Owner: Pavlo Radiuk.** Day 03 topic. Funded by the **Ministry of Education and Science of Ukraine**.
One-pager: `Source_Files/04_OnePager_KhNU_Polymer-Waste-Management.pdf`.

A smart thermochemical system converting polymer waste into liquid fuel, with an
**energy-autonomous solar power supply**. Core components:

- optimisation of thermochemical conversion to maximise useful fuel output and minimise harmful by-products;
- an **automated control system** that adapts the process to different waste types and available solar energy;
- **real-time environmental monitoring** — emissions control and fuel-quality assessment;
- utilisation of by-products along circular-economy principles.

Key outcome: a **decision-support framework for local authorities** to assess the feasibility of
decentralised waste-to-energy solutions at community level.

Municipal angle: decentralised waste processing reduces dependence on large centralised
infrastructure and contributes to local energy resilience — relevant to cities seeking sustainable
waste management, energy autonomy and circular-economy approaches.

Discussion points KhNU wants: municipal approaches to waste processing and circular economy;
perspectives on decentralised waste-to-energy; areas for knowledge exchange or pilot cooperation.

### 1.3 Khmelnytskyi Solid Waste Project — EBRD Operation No. 50729 (City / Smart Environment Office)
**Owner: Yevheniia Smoliienko.** Day 03 topic.
One-pager: `Source_Files/05_OnePager_City_Smart-Environment-Khmelnytskyi.pdf` ·
https://www.ebrd.com/home/work-with-us/projects/psd/50729.html

City of Khmelnytskyi + KCE "Spetskomuntrans" with the EBRD (GrCF2 W2). Addresses a **60-year-old

**Components:** closure and rehabilitation of the existing non-engineered landfill · two new
EU-compliant sanitary landfill cells · Material Recovery Facility (MRF) and composting facility ·
expansion of source separation · neighbourhood Waste Management Centres.

**Recent status (2024–2025):** Leachate Treatment Plant (200 m³/day) in test operation from
05.03.2025, by ARIONEX LT and Rotreat Abwasserreinigung GmbH, **€2.43 m** · MRF & composting
facility retender announced 02.05.2025, evaluation ongoing · landfill rehabilitation and new cells
**€11.39 m** incl. **€5.05 m** grant, works ongoing.

**Already operating:** landfill gas extraction and electricity generation (since 2018) · "Ecobus"
hazardous-waste collection (since 2018) · two Waste Management Centres, a third in preparation ·
the **"Gufi Center"** waste-management training centre (opened 2021).

## 2. What LYKUML asked to see

From Inna Shevchuk, 13.07.2026 (thread `19f4102e9e1a9fcb`) — LYKUML's declared priorities:

- environmental sustainability of the city and climate-change mitigation: the urban-agglomeration experience
- "green" and decentralised generation while keeping access to reliable modern energy in the community/households
- urban planning in the context of comprehensive restoration of historical centres, plus community involvement in environmental protection
- energy efficiency of buildings and the **"15-minute city"** concept
- involving young people in "green"-technology and energy-efficiency start-ups
- business incubators supporting climate-neutrality innovation
- innovative closed-loop business models based on circular-economy and energy-efficiency principles

Institutions LYKUML wanted to meet: **TU Dresden International Office** (Peter Rosenbaum) ·
**UNU-FLORES** · **DRESDEN-concept** · **TUD|excite** (the "TUD Innovation Hub").

**On waste management (for Yevheniia Smoliienko), LYKUML asked about:**
- organisation of a modern municipal waste-management system; practical case studies of waste-collection companies; what happens to residual waste after separate collection (landfill, incineration)
- training and education: environment-related training/education centres including waste management; programmes in educational institutions; resident awareness-raising
- reuse practices, reuse centres and repair workshops
- whether **Zero Waste** principles are implemented, and how
- application of **green public procurement**

**Outcome:** the TU Dresden International Office session made it into the final Day-02 programme.
**UNU-FLORES, DRESDEN-concept and TUD|excite did not** — the planned parallel Tuesday
waste/academic track was cancelled.

## 5. Intended outcomes

The programme is framed as *"Strengthening cooperation through the Dresden–Stuttgart–Khmelnytskyi
Solidarity Partnership and the EU-funded project U_CAN"*. Working themes Radka has consistently
pushed:

- identify concrete synergies rather than general goodwill — she repeatedly asked partners to name **specific stakeholders, chairs and projects**;
- open a path to **long-term academic and research cooperation** between KhNU/LYKUML and TU Dresden (the International Office session exists for exactly this);
- give the Khmelnytskyi City Council delegation value **beyond U_CAN**, in the broader Solidarity Partnership;
- produce communicable results — the *"3 things Dresden can learn from Ukraine — and vice versa"* idea is a direct request to distil concrete lessons from the three days.
