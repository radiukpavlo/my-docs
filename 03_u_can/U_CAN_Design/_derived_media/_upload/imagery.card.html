Photography

All six pilot cities now have photography. Sourced from the consortium communications library (WP7_Communication, dissemination, exploitation/Pictures/) and supplied in the three crops below. They are aerial and street cityscapes with no identifiable individuals, so they carry no consent burden and can be published as they are.

Pilot-city heroes — 16:9

Each city also ships -card-8x5.jpg and -square-1x1.jpg, centre-cropped from the same original. Never upscaled — the delivered size is capped by the source. Ivano-Frankivsk is print quality (5184 × 3349 original). Lviv is the thin one: exactly one photograph of Lviv exists in the whole archive, so its hero tops out at 1024 px and should not be used full-bleed above that width.

Official pilot-city banners — and a contrast problem in three of them

Six banners, 1440 × 810, one per pilot city, produced by WP7. Each carries the reversed mark with a circular city photograph masked into the wordmark, the city name, the pilot's one-line objective and the white EU emblem, on a per-city background colour. The backgrounds are the brand palette, which is worth recording as a real per-city coding — and one of them introduces an orange the token set does not contain.

Three of the six set white body text on a ground that cannot carry it, and the Vinnytsia banner is the exact pairing this system forbids — white on --ucan-yellow, 1.51:1, the worst measurable combination in the palette. See guidelines/colors-pairings.card.html. The objective line under the city name is small type, so Zhytomyr's 3.97:1 fails for it too.
How to use them anyway. The banners are good artwork — official, on-brand, correctly cropped, and the only place the reversed lockup appears in real use. Treat the text baked into them as decorative: on any surface that must be accessible, place the city name and objective as real HTML over the banner, or beside it, rather than relying on the pixels. For the three failing cities that also means not reusing their baked-in caption at all. The clean fix is to ask WP7 to reissue the three with --ucan-navy text — navy on yellow measures 8.77:1, navy on the green 5.17:1.
Where a surface supplies its own caption and lockup, use the plain -hero-16x9 / -card-8x5 crops instead; they carry no text and no contrast liability.

⚠️ Consent — the binding rule

Of ten curated project photographs in the archive, only two contain no identifiable people. Those two are published here. The other eight show faces at close range — conference panels, a posed delegation line-up, briefing and reception tables — and are not in assets/. They stay out until a release is on file for every person shown. A public event does not imply consent for marketing use.
Still needed: 4–8 more project photographs, once consent is confirmed for the eight already selected, or fresh ones shot with releases. The eight are staged outside the system with per-photo notes in _derived_media/MEDIA-MANIFEST.csv.

The photographic style

AttributeRule
ColourFull colour, cool-neutral. Not monochrome — the brand's own palette is saturated, and a mono library fights the green and blue fields it sits next to.
SubjectPeople doing the work, in real places: workshops, site visits, municipal offices, installations. Not empty architecture, not abstract "technology" imagery. Cityscapes are the exception — they carry the pilot-city surfaces, where the city itself is the subject.
FramingCandid over posed. A posed line-up is acceptable for a consortium record shot and nowhere else.
ContextUkrainian and EU settings should both be visibly present across the set — the project is about the exchange between them.
Crop16:9 heroes, 8:5 cards, 1:1 portraits. Faces must survive the 1:1 crop.
Type over imageOnly with --scrim-strong. Measured, that is 5.67:1 against a pure-white photo; --scrim-medium reaches 3.45:1 and is headline scale only.
Alt textDescribe what is happening and where. "Aerial view of the city hall square in Zhytomyr", not "photo" or "image1.jpg".
Consent and licenceEvery photograph of an identifiable person needs consent on file, and the licence recorded in the asset manifest. No stock, ever.
